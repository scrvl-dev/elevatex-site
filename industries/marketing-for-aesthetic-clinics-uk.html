Why most aesthetic clinics marketing fails

Sound familiar?

The four problems we see in nearly every aesthetic clinics engagement audit. Each is independently fixable. Compounded together, they're the difference between an acquisition system that scales and one that stalls.

01

Lead response speed is killing your conversion rate

Industry data is clear: patients who get a response within five minutes book at five times the rate of those who wait an hour. Most clinics respond in 4-12 hours. AI-handled response within 60 seconds is the difference between filling your treatment chairs and watching them sit empty.

02

Generic Meta ads keep getting rejected (or worse, you get account-banned)

The CAP code on cosmetic procedures is strict. Showing dramatic before/after results, making clinical claims, using superlatives — any of these can get an ad rejected, an account flagged, or in the worst case, an Advertising Standards Authority complaint. Most freelance media buyers don't know the rules. We do.

03

You're spending £50+ per consultation and your competitors are at £15

If your CPL is high and not dropping, the problem is almost always upstream of the ad budget. Wrong creative, wrong audiences, wrong landing pages, wrong tracking. We diagnose and fix that systematically — our last clinic engagement dropped CPL from materially, by fixing what sits upstream of the ad account.

04

Your booking system loses leads in the gaps

Patient enquires → admin sees enquiry → admin emails back → patient takes a day to reply → admin offers a time → patient says no, prefers Tuesday → back and forth for a week → patient ghosts. The gaps in this process are where revenue dies. We close them.

Channel breakdown

Which channels actually work
for aesthetic clinics

Not every channel works in every niche. Here's where the money goes — and where it doesn't — across our aesthetic clinics engagements.

01

Meta Ads (Instagram + Facebook)

Carries the majority of new-patient acquisition for most clinics. Instagram heavily outperforms Facebook for aesthetics specifically — the audience visual context matters. Creative needs to be UGC-feel rather than produced. Ad copy must comply with CAP code: no 'before and after' wording paired with dramatic transformations, no superlatives, no medical claims.

02

Google Search Ads

Captures patients actively searching for specific treatments in their area. 'Lip filler London', 'Botox near me', 'skin booster Manchester'. Higher intent than Meta, higher CPL, but the highest-converting traffic in the funnel.

03

Google Business Profile

Free. Massively under-utilised. Optimised GBPs with regular posts, treatment photos, and review management drive significant local search traffic at zero cost. Setup and ongoing optimisation is included in most engagements.

04

Local SEO

Long-term play. Treatment-specific landing pages targeting 'treatment + location' keywords compound over 6-12 months. We don't lead with this for clinics under £1M revenue because the payback is too long — but for established clinics it's a foundational layer.

05

Referral programmes

Existing-patient referrals are the highest-quality and lowest-cost acquisition channel for clinics. Most have no formal programme. We build the automation and incentive structure that turns happy patients into a steady referral pipeline.

06

Reactivation campaigns

Patients who haven't booked in 6-12 months represent the cheapest revenue you can generate. Win-back sequences with treatment-anniversary incentives reliably recover 10-15% of dormant patient lists.

Regulation

CAP code & UK aesthetic marketing compliance

Regulatory awareness isn't optional in this niche. Here's what we factor into every campaign, landing page, and creative we ship.

Compliance considerations we build around

  • The Committee of Advertising Practice (CAP) code governs all advertising of cosmetic procedures in the UK. Marketing that breaches the code can result in Advertising Standards Authority adjudications, social media account suspensions, and reputational damage that takes years to recover from.
  • Before-and-after imagery is the most commonly breached area. CAP rules require comparable lighting, angles, and conditions; no makeup differences; no exaggerated transformations; and clear disclaimer that individual results vary. We design creative that satisfies these rules while still being commercially effective.
  • Prescription-only medicines (POMs) including Botox cannot be advertised directly to the public. Marketing must reference the treatment context (e.g. 'anti-wrinkle treatment') rather than the brand name. Most freelance media buyers get this wrong; we have the language patterns documented.
  • JCCP and Save Face registration is a trust signal increasingly important to discerning patients. Where applicable we incorporate badging into landing pages and ad creative as a conversion lift.
