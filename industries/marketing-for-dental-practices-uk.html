Why most dental practices marketing fails

Sound familiar?

The four problems we see in nearly every dental practices engagement audit. Each is independently fixable. Compounded together, they're the difference between an acquisition system that scales and one that stalls.

01

Your website is invisible on Google for the queries that matter

When patients search 'Invisalign near me' or 'dental implants Brighton', your practice doesn't appear. The five clinics that do appear are taking your patients every single month. Most practices have never invested seriously in local SEO or paid search; the ones that do dominate their geography for years.

02

Your front desk loses leads on phone and email enquiries

An enquiry comes in at 6pm asking about Invisalign pricing. Nobody answers until 9am the next day. By then, the patient has called two competitors and booked a consultation with whoever responded fastest. AI receptionists fix this; manual phone handling does not.

03

You don't have treatment specific funnels for high margin services

Invisalign is a £3,500 service. Composite bonding is £400. Whitening is £300. These need separate landing pages, separate ad creative, separate qualification flows. Most practices treat them all the same on a single 'book a consultation' page and convert 1 to 2% of traffic instead of 8 to 12%.

04

Your dormant patient list is bigger than your active list, and you do nothing with it

Every practice has 2 to 3x as many patients on the books who haven't been in for 18+ months as they have active patients. That's the cheapest revenue source available. Most practices have zero reactivation programme. The right automated sequence recovers 10 to 15% of dormant patients within 60 days.

Regulation

GDC, CQC & UK dental marketing compliance

Regulatory awareness isn't optional in this niche. Here's what we factor into every campaign, landing page, and creative we ship.

Compliance considerations we build around

  • General Dental Council (GDC) Standards for the Dental Team govern all advertising and marketing by registered dental professionals. Marketing must not mislead, must not make unverifiable claims, and must not exploit patients' lack of knowledge.
  • Before and after imagery is governed by GDC and CAP code. Must be the same patient. Must be comparable lighting and angle. Must include treatment timeline and disclaimer that individual results vary.
  • Care Quality Commission (CQC) registration is required for all primary care dental services in England. CQC registration details should be present on the practice website and marketing materials.
  • Pricing transparency is increasingly important from a regulatory and trust perspective. Practices that publish price ranges (with appropriate 'from' disclaimers) on landing pages typically outconvert those that hide pricing behind 'contact us for a quote' barriers.
