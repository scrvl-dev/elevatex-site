The UK renewable energy installer market is booming and broken at the same time. Booming because the BUS grant (£7,500 toward heat pump installation), ECO4 obligations, and rising energy prices have created the strongest demand signal in the industry's history. Broken because the lead-generation ecosystem is dominated by aggregators who sell the same homeowner enquiry to seven different installers, each of whom calls the homeowner within an hour and burns the customer relationship before it begins.

The installers winning right now have stopped buying leads. They've built their own customer-acquisition systems. They run Google Search for high-intent queries like 'air source heat pump grant' and 'solar panels Newcastle'. They run paid social with creative built around real installations, not stock photos. They have AI-handled enquiry qualification that converts at 3x the rate of phone tag. They own the customer relationship from first ad click to installation day and beyond.

ElevateX builds those systems. Below: the specific problems renewable installers face right now, what we ship to solve them, the regulatory and MCS landscape, and the kind of installer this works best for.

Why most renewable energy installers marketing fails

Sound familiar?

The four problems we see in nearly every renewable energy installers engagement audit. Each is independently fixable. Compounded together, they're the difference between an acquisition system that scales and one that stalls.

01

You're paying £80-£250 per 'lead' to aggregators who sell it to six competitors

The lead aggregator model is broken. Most installers we audit are paying £100-£200 per lead, converting 5-10% of them, and ending up with effective cost-per-installation of £1,500-£3,000. Owned acquisition through proper paid search, paid social, and SEO consistently produces installations at £300-£800 each.

02

The BUS grant and ECO4 opportunity is moving faster than your marketing can keep up

Government incentive schemes change quarterly. ECO4 obligations shift between providers. BUS grant amounts adjust. Most installers find out about changes through trade press or supplier emails and lose 30-60 days of marketing relevance. We track the policy landscape so your ads and landing pages reflect current scheme details from day one of any change.

03

Your sales cycle is 8-14 weeks and you have nothing nurturing leads through it

Heat pump and solar installations are considered purchases. Homeowners take 6-14 weeks from first enquiry to signed contract. Most installers respond to the initial enquiry, follow up twice, and then go silent. The leads that quietly convert with a competitor were yours to keep — you just had nothing in place to keep them warm.

04

MCS certification is your competitive moat and your marketing barely mentions it

MCS certification is required for BUS grant access and is a meaningful trust signal for homeowners. Most installer websites mention it once in a footer. The installers who lead with their MCS credentials in their hero sections, treat it as a brand pillar, and explain why it matters convert significantly higher.

Regulation

MCS, OFGEM & UK renewable installer marketing compliance

Regulatory awareness isn't optional in this niche. Here's what we factor into every campaign, landing page, and creative we ship.

Compliance considerations we build around

  • MCS (Microgeneration Certification Scheme) certification is required for BUS grant eligibility and is a meaningful consumer trust signal. Marketing should prominently display MCS membership and explain why it matters for homeowners.
  • Government scheme details (BUS grant amounts, ECO4 eligibility criteria, scheme deadlines) shift quarterly. Marketing claims must be current. Outdated scheme references create both consumer complaints and ASA exposure.
  • Energy savings and bill-reduction claims must be evidenced and qualified. 'Save up to 70% on your energy bills' style claims require both substantiation and 'individual results vary' disclaimers. ASA pays attention to this category.
  • Trustmark, RECC (Renewable Energy Consumer Code), and HIES membership where held should be displayed alongside MCS as trust stack signals. Homeowners researching make a buying decision partly based on regulatory credibility.
